RETURN TO THE HONEY JAR

One industry, shaped by people, place, landscape and trade.

Australia's honey industry is more complex than the jar on the shelf suggests. Beekeeping activity is concentrated unevenly across the country, with New South Wales and Victoria supporting large beekeeper communities while states differ substantially in hive scale and dependence on public land.

At the same time, Australia's position in the honey trade has changed. What was once a strong export surplus increasingly shifted towards import pressure, with trade deficits emerging in several recent years. Together, these patterns show that the industry's future depends not only on beekeepers, but also on access to suitable landscapes and its changing position in international trade.

Behind every honey jar is a network of people, places and economic relationships stretching across Australia.
